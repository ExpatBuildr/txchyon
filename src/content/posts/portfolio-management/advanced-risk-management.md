## Stop-Losses: The First Line of Defense (Not a Magic Button)

Stop-losses are **predefined exit points** designed to prevent small losses from becoming catastrophic ones.

They exist for one reason:

> To make sure one bad move doesn’t end your entire strategy.

### Common Stop-Loss Types (Simply Explained)

**Fixed Stops**  
Sell if price drops X%.  
Simple. Brutal. Effective.

**Trailing Stops**  
Move upward as price rises.  
Protects gains during strong trends.

**Volatility-Based Stops**  
Wider stops for wild assets, tighter for calm ones.  
This prevents getting shaken out of normal noise.

Stop-losses are tools — not guarantees.  
They work best when paired with **allocation discipline and rebalancing**.

## Drawdowns: The Silent Portfolio Killer

A drawdown is how far your portfolio falls from its peak.

And here’s the brutal truth:

- A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to recover  
- A 70% drawdown requires a 233% gain  

Most portfolios never come back from deep drawdowns — emotionally or mathematically.

### Drawdown Management Is About Staying Functional

Good drawdown control:
- reduces emotional decisions  
- preserves optionality  
- keeps you liquid  

Bad drawdown control:
- traps capital  
- forces panic selling  
- kills compounding  

This is why **allocation and volatility control matter more than moonshots**.

## Hedging: Reducing Risk Without Selling Everything

Hedging answers a powerful question:

> “How do I protect downside *without* exiting my core positions?”

### Common Crypto Hedging Methods

**Stablecoin Buffers**  
Parking part of the portfolio in USDC/USDT during uncertainty.

**Futures Shorts**  
Offset long exposure with small short positions.

**Cross-Asset Hedging**  
Reducing alt exposure while maintaining BTC.

Hedging is not about being bearish —  
it’s about **controlling exposure when risk spikes**.

Most investors hedge too late or too aggressively.  
The sweet spot is **partial, temporary, and rule-based**.

---

## Correlation Risk: Why “Diversified” Portfolios Still Blow Up

Holding 12 altcoins does NOT mean you’re diversified.

If everything drops together — you weren’t diversified.  
You were **correlated**.

Correlation risk happens when:
- assets respond to the same macro forces  
- narratives collapse together  
- liquidity exits the same door  

Smart portfolios reduce correlation by:
- mixing asset types  
- balancing chains  
- avoiding theme overload  

This is especially important in multi-chain portfolios.

## Value at Risk (VaR): Measuring Pain Before It Happens

VaR answers a calm but powerful question:

> “How bad could this realistically get?”

Example:
> “There’s a 5% chance I lose more than X% in a month.”

VaR doesn’t predict crashes.  
It **quantifies exposure**.

Why it matters:
- sets position size limits  
- informs hedging decisions  
- prevents overconfidence  

Think of VaR as a **speedometer for risk**, not a crystal ball.
